Minamo v0.1.19 lands the two deferred drummer-mode prototype gates — calibrated
two-camera fusion (#241) and the BLE drum-stick profile (#240) — and corrects
three measurement or documentation defects those prototypes surfaced.

Both features remain optional and out of MVP. The single-webcam, no-accessory
path is unchanged and remains the default.

## Two-camera drum fusion prototype (#241)

- `src/core/multiCamera.ts` adds extrinsic calibration into a shared stage
  frame, ray triangulation, capture-clock alignment, and explicit rejection of
  stale, uncalibrated, frame-incompatible, non-finite and disagreeing sources.
- Two layers with different depth rules: per-frame trajectories require two
  views and reject a single view rather than guessing depth; hit candidates
  accept one view, resolved against the calibrated head plane where that
  constraint is valid.
- Candidates are matched across cameras by ray agreement rather than by the
  zone label each camera reported, so two cameras that disagree about the zone
  are settled geometrically instead of being counted twice.
- `pnpm bench:multicam` is a deterministic A/B harness. Measured on synthetic
  material: recall 0.600 to 1.000 with a second camera, all 16 occluded strokes
  recovered, and a triangulated track accurate to 5.7 mm mean / 10.5 mm p95.
- Disconnecting the second camera reproduces the single-camera baseline exactly.

## BLE drum-stick profile (#240)

- `src/core/bleMidiStick.ts` adds a BLE-MIDI decoder, GM percussion note
  mapping, capability-based transport selection, a reconnect duplicate guard,
  and device-identifier redaction.
- The supported profile is BLE-MIDI (MMA/AMEI RP-052), not a vendor GATT
  service: shipping sticks run impact detection on-device and transmit
  note-on with velocity rather than raw accelerometer or gyro samples.
- Web Bluetooth is not required. An OS-paired BLE-MIDI device appears as an
  ordinary MIDI port, so the browser reaches it through Web MIDI, and the
  desktop bridge becomes a MIDI bridge rather than a BLE GATT bridge.
- Stick timing and velocity fuse with camera position; a stroke seen by only
  one source passes through unchanged in both directions, so an accessory can
  never remove a hit the camera already had.
- The decoder degrades rather than throwing, and is fuzzed with 20,000 seeded
  random packets.

## Corrections

- `scoreDrumBenchmarkEvents` matched expected hits to detections by time alone,
  so on any groove with two limbs on one beat a backbeat snare could consume the
  hi-hat's detection. It now reserves same-zone pairs before matching across
  zones. Previously recorded zone and hand accuracy for such clips were
  pessimistic.
- The #278 MIDI adapter guidance prescribed degrading to "MIDI is unavailable on
  this browser" and staying there. That is correct for Safari and wrong for
  Firefox, where `requestMIDIAccess()` rejects until a site permission add-on is
  installed even though feature detection passes.
- `DrumHitDetector` re-fires every `cooldownMs` throughout a descent, because a
  zone is a sphere whose radius exceeds the height a stick is lifted, so the
  rebound re-arm added in #123 never engages. This is recorded and reproducible
  via `pnpm bench:multicam`, and is deliberately **not** fixed in this release:
  changing when a hit fires trades against the fast-roll behaviour #123
  protects and needs real clips to tune. Tracked on #235 and #121.

## Known limitations

- Both prototypes are gated on hardware that has not been run. #241 needs two
  real cameras, consented clips and measured drift; #240 needs a physical stick
  for packet rate, loss, reconnect timing, velocity correlation and battery
  drain. Neither issue is closed by this release.
- The two-camera A/B measured zone accuracy at 1.000 for both one and two
  cameras, because the synthetic kit's zones project far apart in the front
  view. On that material the second camera's only measured benefit is occlusion
  recovery.
- Windows and macOS installers still lack publicly trusted developer
  certificates. SmartScreen or macOS Privacy & Security approval may be
  required even though in-app update artifacts are cryptographically verified.
- macOS packages are ad-hoc signed but not notarized; Linux packages are not
  GPG-signed.
